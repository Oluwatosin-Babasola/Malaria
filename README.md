# Malaria
### Simulation study for methodological validation
A simulation study is conducted to evaluate the ability of the proposed hierarchical model to recover latent malaria incidence and transmission metrics under imperfect surveillance. The primary objective is to quantify the bias introduced by underreporting and to assess the extent to which the proposed correction framework reduces this bias.
The use of simulated data is necessary because true incidence and reporting probability are not directly observable in real surveillance systems. By constructing a synthetic dataset with known ground truth, it becomes possible to evaluate estimation accuracy, assess robustness under varying reporting conditions, and quantify the impact of correction on downstream quantities such as the time varying reproduction number.
The simulation framework is designed to reproduce key epidemiological and surveillance characteristics observed in malaria endemic settings, including spatial heterogeneity in transmission, environmental seasonality, and structured reporting bias linked to health system performance.
### Data generating process
The synthetic dataset is generated using a two stage process that separates transmission dynamics from the observation mechanism. Let I_(i,t) denote the true number of malaria cases in spatial unit i at time t, and let Y_(i,t) denote the observed reported cases.
The observation process is defined as
Y_(i,t)∼"Poisson"(p_(i,t) " " I_(i,t))

where p_(i,t)∈(0,1) represents the reporting probability. This corresponds to a Poisson thinning formulation of underreporting, which preserves stochastic variability in incidence while explicitly modeling incomplete observation.
A.3 Simulation of latent incidence
The latent incidence process is constructed to reflect realistic spatial and temporal variation in malaria transmission. Incidence is driven by population size, baseline transmission intensity, and environmental forcing.
For each spatial unit i, a baseline parasite prevalence proxy 〖"PfPR" 〗_i is assigned to represent long term transmission intensity. This spatial pattern is designed to reflect empirical malaria risk distributions, with higher values in regions such as the Lake Victoria basin and lower values in highland areas.

### Simulation of reporting probability
Reporting probability is modeled as a function of surveillance system characteristics and health system access. The logit of the reporting probability is specified as
$logit(p_(i,t))=β_0+β_1 C_(i,t)+β_2 T_(i,t)+β_3 H_i+β_4 A_i+β_5 S_(i,t)+ϵ_(i,t)$

where C_(i,t)  represents reporting completeness, T_(i,t )reporting timeliness, H_i  health facility density, A_i geographic accessibility, and S_(i,t) indicators of integrated NTD program activity. The term ϵ_(i,t) represents unobserved variability.
This formulation reflects empirical evidence that reporting performance depends on accessibility, infrastructure, and health system capacity. Spatial heterogeneity in these variables produces realistic variation in reporting probability across regions.
A.5 Construction of observed surveillance data
Observed case counts are generated by combining the latent incidence process and the reporting model. For each spatial unit and time point, observed cases are drawn from the Poisson thinning process defined in Section A.2.
This procedure produces a dataset that mimics routine surveillance data, with spatially structured underreporting and temporal variability in reporting performance. The resulting data exhibit key features observed in real systems, including lower reporting in remote areas and fluctuations associated with changes in system performance.

### Alignment with methodological literature
The simulation framework is consistent with established approaches in infectious disease modeling that treat observed data as a partially observed realization of a latent transmission process. Similar hierarchical constructions have been used in studies of underreporting and epidemic reconstruction, where explicit modeling of the observation process is necessary to obtain unbiased estimates of transmission metrics.
The use of Poisson thinning is standard in this context because it provides a tractable representation of incomplete reporting while preserving the stochastic nature of incidence.

### Evaluation metrics
The performance of the proposed model is evaluated using several quantitative measures.
First, reconstruction accuracy is assessed by comparing estimated incidence I ̂_(i,t)  with true incidence I_(i,t). Metrics such as bias, root mean squared error, and coverage of credible intervals are used to quantify estimation accuracy.
Second, reporting probability estimates p ̂_(i,t)  are compared with true values to assess the ability of the model to recover spatial and temporal variation in reporting.
Third, the impact of correction on transmission inference is evaluated by comparing reproduction numbers computed from true incidence, observed incidence, and reconstructed incidence. This allows direct quantification of the bias introduced by imperfect surveillance and the extent to which it is reduced by the model.

### Simulated data
Although the dataset is synthetic, it is designed to reproduce key empirical characteristics of malaria transmission and surveillance systems. These include spatial heterogeneity consistent with prevalence mapping studies, seasonal variation driven by environmental forcing, nonlinear relationships between prevalence and incidence, and structured reporting bias linked to health system access.
By incorporating these features, the simulation provides a testbed for evaluating methodological performance while maintaining full control over the underlying processes.
